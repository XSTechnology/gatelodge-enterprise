Survey the estate.

Most agent projects fail because someone started building before understanding the estate. We don't. We walk your workflows, map your decision points, and re-engineer the process before we write agent code.

We document your existing workflows end-to-end — every person, system, document, and handoff involved. Not the org-chart version; the version that actually happens on a Tuesday.

What we're looking for

  • Who does what, with what information, in what order
  • Where work stalls: queues, approvals, handoffs, rework loops
  • Which systems hold the information people need to do their jobs
  • What's measured today vs. what actually matters

Deliverable

Annotated process map with friction points scored by frequency × cost × agent-replaceability

Why it matters

→ Before we design anything, we know where the gates in your estate actually are — and which ones are worth automating first.

Map the gates.

↺ the estate evolves — walk feeds the next survey

Every workflow has decision points — moments where a human judges, approves, routes, or escalates. We identify each one and score whether an agent can hold it.

What we're looking for

  • What information feeds the decision (documents, data, context)
  • What happens on each branch — and the cost of getting it wrong
  • Which decisions are routine (high-volume, pattern-based) vs. high-stakes (regulatory, relational, irrevocable)
  • What "good enough" looks like — the threshold for agent confidence

Deliverable

Decision-point inventory with agent-replaceability scoring and information-source mapping

Why it matters

→ Every gate gets a verdict: agent-ready, human-only, or hybrid (agent drafts, human approves). The hybrid gates are usually the highest ROI.

We re-engineer the workflow: which gates become agentic loops, which stay human, and how the two hand off cleanly.

What we're designing

  • The agent's knowledge base — what it knows, what it looks up, what it admits it doesn't know
  • The confidence threshold — below it, escalate; above it, act
  • The escalation path — who gets notified, with what context, and how they respond
  • The routing plan — which model handles which task, with what budget, and what happens when the budget runs out
  • The audit specification — what gets logged, how it's chained, who can verify it

Deliverable

Re-engineered process with agent architecture, routing plan, guardrail specification, and escalation design

Why it matters

→ This is where most consultancies stop. We keep going — because the design isn't real until it's running on governed infrastructure.
