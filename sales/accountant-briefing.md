## The agenda — eight questions

1. **Corporate residency.** The corporation is Delaware-formed but managed
   and controlled from Canada. Is it US-resident, Canadian-resident, or
   dual-resident? Which treaty tiebreaker applies, and what does the answer
   change? *This is the root question — it shapes 2–7.*
2. **Canadian permanent establishment.** If US-resident: does the owner's
   home office (single director, no employees) create a PE in Canada for the
   US corporation under the treaty?
3. **Delaware franchise tax.** Authorized-shares vs assumed-par-value method
   given [SHARE COUNT]; expected annual amount; is amending the certificate
   to reduce authorized shares worthwhile? (Annual report + tax due March 1.)
4. **US federal filings.** Form 1120 cadence/first-year obligations; the
   **annual Form 5472 + pro-forma 1120 requirement for a foreign-owned US
   corporation (100% foreign shareholder) — including with zero revenue;
   $25,000 penalty for missing it.** Who prepares these and on what calendar?
5. **GST/HST.** Obligations on digital/software sales to Canadian customers:
   resident-supplier vs non-resident-supplier rules (given the residency
   answer to Q1), registration threshold, place of supply, QST if Quebec
   buyers. Practical recommendation for a small seller.
6. **Other jurisdictions.** Any obligations the corporation should know
   about for EU/UK B2C digital sales (VAT), and is Stripe Tax the pragmatic
   way to handle it?
7. **Extraction.** Salary vs dividends from the US corporation to a
   Canadian-resident sole shareholder; CPP vs US social security
   (totalization agreement); T1135 foreign-property reporting once share
   cost exceeds CAD 100,000. What keeps annual compliance simplest?
8. **Go-live checklist.** Given the above: anything to fix **before** the
   first live dollar (banking, elections, elections-out-of-window, GST
   registration, invoicing posture)? The intent is to switch Stripe to live
   mode shortly after this consult.
